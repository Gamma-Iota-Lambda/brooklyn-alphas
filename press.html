About Gamma Iota Lambda

Gamma Iota Lambda Chapter, the Brooklyn Alphas, is a graduate chapter of Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Inc., the first intercollegiate Greek-letter fraternity established for African American men. Chartered in Brooklyn on May 5, 1945, the chapter develops leaders, promotes brotherhood and academic excellence, and provides service and advocacy for communities in Brooklyn and Long Island. Its programs include the Alpha Academy mentoring program, an annual MLK Day of Service and the Judge Myles A. Paige Scholarship. Learn more at www.gil1906.org.

Programs

Alpha Academy

Leadership and college readiness program for young men of color in grades 9 to 12 at Brooklyn schools. Meets one Saturday a month during the school year.

Alpha Academy

MLK Day of Service

Held each January since 2015. Free haircuts, hot meals, clothing drives and community health screenings across Brooklyn.

Judge Myles A. Paige Scholarship

Annual award for graduating male high school seniors who live or attend school in Brooklyn and are headed to college full time.

Scholarship

Impact $20

A giving program that funds Alpha Academy, community education initiatives and scholarships. Gifts are tax-deductible through the Gamma Iota Lambda Foundation, Inc.

History in brief

On Saturday, May 5, 1945, eleven men chartered Gamma Iota Lambda in Brooklyn. It became the second alumni chapter of Alpha Phi Alpha in New York City. Charter member Myles A. Paige later served as the fraternity's 19th General President. In 2025 the chapter marked its 80th anniversary, and its papers were accepted into the Center for Brooklyn History.
